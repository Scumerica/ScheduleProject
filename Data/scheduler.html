Secondary (ghost) roles

A secondary role is carried on top of someone's main job, for example CRC for typists who may be pulled to the front. It takes no station, is not part of the hierarchy and does not count toward staffing or appointments. Each week its Required number of people are picked from those whose main job is one of the positions ticked below, on a rotation of its own (Max Rotation and Break Rotation) that leaves the main positions' rotation alone. Set its quotas in the position list.

How Scheduling Works
A plain-language walk through what the scheduler does and why.

The short version

Once a week the scheduler hands out the special duties. Each day it only adjusts for who is out. A person normally does the same job, at the same station, with the same breaks, for the whole week.

When it has a choice, it picks whoever has waited longest for a turn. When it does not have enough people, it fills the most important roles first and tells you where it had to bend a rule.

Your current settings

1. What you tell it

  • Who can do what. The duty checkboxes on each employee card. Typing means they can work a typing station.
  • Who can sit at each station. On each station card, the Typing and Info Desk ticks say who may sit there. The ticks for the other positions mark a preferred station: someone on that duty is seated there when it is free, and otherwise takes any free typing station. Banker, Class C/M and CDL are seated after the typing staff, so they never take a station a typist needs. These ticks do not limit which appointments or transactions a station serves.
  • How many of each role you need. The Required number for each role, per day.
  • Who is out, and when. Staff time off (whole days or part of a day) is added and removed on the Office Calendar. Employee cards show it for the week picked on their calendar button, but it cannot be changed there.

2. At the start of each week

  1. Count the openings. Each role needs its Required number of people, less anyone frozen in that role.
  2. Supervisor overrides come first. Anyone you gave a duty for the week (the pencil button on the schedule, or Assigned Duty on their card) gets it, even if they are resting, over the Fairness Duty Limiter or not ticked for it.
  3. Anyone who owes a week goes next. A person who held a role but was out three or more days of that week is put back on the same role.
  4. Fixed-station staff take a role their station allows, if one is open and they can be picked.
  5. The rest are filled in hierarchy order. Higher roles pick before lower ones. Roles marked equal take turns, one position each.
  6. The fairest person is picked. Among those who can be picked, the one who has gone longest without holding any weekly position goes first. Ties go to whoever has worked the fewest special-duty days overall.
  7. Everyone gets a station. Position holders get a station that allows their role. Everyone else gets a typing station, and those who have been left Unassigned most often get one first.

3. Who can be picked

For a role in a given week, a person must:

  • be ticked for that duty,
  • not be resting from that role,
  • be under the Fairness Duty Limiter,
  • be out no more than two days that week,
  • not be frozen, and not be marked "No special duty" for the week.

If nobody meets all of these, the scheduler does not leave the role empty. It takes the person closest to finishing their rest, then someone over the Fairness Duty Limiter, and shows a warning naming them.

4. Each day

  1. Anyone marked out shows as Out.
  2. Frozen employees keep their position.
  3. One-day overrides are applied. A person you overrode for that day takes the duty you chose; whoever loses the position works a typing station for the day.
  4. Position holders work their role at their own station.
  5. If a holder is out, a stand-in covers for that day only. The stand-in comes from the holder's rotation group when possible, is chosen by the same fairness order, uses the holder's station, and goes back to their own job the next day.
  6. Everyone else works their typing station. A person without one takes a station that is free that day, or shows as Unassigned.

5. Rest, limits and rotation

  • Max Rotation is the most days in a row on one role. With 5, a person does one week and rotates off. With 10, they may stay a second week.
  • Break Rotation is how many days a person rests from that role afterwards. Only days they are present count, so a day out does not shorten the rest.
  • The Fairness Duty Limiter caps special-duty days across all roles, so nobody goes from one duty straight into another week after week.
  • The call-out rule. A holder who is out three or more days of their week earns no rest and gets the same role again the next week.
  • Rest that cannot be sustained is shortened. If a rest period is longer than the number of qualified people can cover, it is cut for that day and a warning says by how much.

6. Rotation groups (breaks and lunch)

Every employee belongs to a rotation group. Each group has a name, its own shift start and end, AM break, lunch and PM break. A title (Supervisor, Lead, Hearing Officer, LPS) can have groups of its own; a title without any uses the groups for Everyone. People in a rotating group move to the next rotating group of their title each week, in the order listed, so nobody keeps the same lunch hour permanently. People in a static group keep the same times every week.

7. Position swaps (after lunch or at a set time)

Swaps are set up under Settings. Each swap has two sides, each holding one or more positions (for example Information Desk on one side, Class C/M + CDL on the other), and its own time; a blank time means when each person's lunch ends. People on one side trade places at that time, one for one, with people on the other. A pair must each be ticked for the other's position, partners in the same break group are matched first, and as many pairs as possible are made. Swaps at the same time are matched in the order listed; a later swap starts from where everyone is after the earlier ones, so a person can swap more than once. Anyone left without a partner is told to check with a supervisor. There are no swaps on a blocked day, no after-lunch swaps on a half day, and no timed swap whose time is blocked.

8. The office calendar

  • A blocked day is nobody's work day. It counts toward no one's duty, rest or days out.
  • Half days and blocked hours close that part of the day for appointments. They are noted on the schedules but do not change who works where.

9. The forecast

The Next Duty column, the 🔜 icon, the 4-Week View and the Employee View are all worked out the same way: the scheduler plays the schedule forward 30 work days on a copy of the roster, using the days off, assigned duties and calendar entries already planned. It updates the moment any of those change.

11. Appointments to publish

Appointments to Publish shows how many appointments of each type to post for each day and each 30 minutes. The numbers come from who the schedule has in each position, 30 minutes at a time, after breaks, lunches, time off and swaps. Nothing there changes who works where.

11. Appointments to publish

The appointment groups in Settings turn the staff schedule into a number of appointments to post for each day. Nothing here changes who works where.

  1. Place the staff, minute by minute. For each group, the people the schedule puts in its ticked positions that day. Typing means anyone at a typing counter. A person is left out while on their break or lunch, on a day out, during any part-day time off set on their card, and before their break group's shift starts or after it ends. A paired Information Desk or Class C/M person counts in their partner's position once their own lunch is over.
  2. Add up each hour. Only the group's hours for the day count (appointment hours or road test hours). A blocked day gives nothing; the blocked half of a half day and any blocked hours are taken out.
  3. Share each hour by weight. Each appointment type or sub-group takes its weight's share. A sub-group adds the minutes of its own ticked positions, if it has any, and shares the total out again.
  4. Divide by the transaction time. An appointment is counted in the hour its minutes are complete, and whatever is left of an hour carries into the next. A day's total is its minutes divided by the time limit, rounded down.

The page shows a whole week by day, or any one day in 30-minute steps. The day table also lists how many people are on break or lunch, off, or not on shift in each 30 minutes.

Tapering. The graph on the page weights each 30 minutes of the day against the others; a point can be set on the hour or the half hour. A day's appointments are spread over its hours by staff time multiplied by the hour's weight, so the number for the day does not change: an hour dragged down gets fewer and the others share what it gave up. With a flat line every hour simply gets what its staff can do. Click an hour to add a point and drag it; double-click a point to remove it. The curve can be the same every day, or a weekday can have its own. A group or a single appointment type can also be given a curve of its own: an appointment type uses its own curve if it has one, otherwise the nearest group above it that has one, otherwise the curve for all appointments.

Weights can be read two ways, chosen in Settings. As a share of appointments, 40% Long and 60% Short posts 4 Long for every 6 Short. As a share of staff time, Long gets 40% of the minutes, which is fewer appointments because each one takes longer.

Weights in a group that add up to less than 100 leave the rest of its time unposted, for walk-ins or catching up. Weights over 100 are scaled down to fit.
