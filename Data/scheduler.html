How Scheduling Works
A plain-language walk through what the scheduler does and why.

The short version

Once a week the scheduler hands out the special duties. Each day it only adjusts for who is out. A person normally does the same job, at the same station, with the same breaks, for the whole week.

When it has a choice, it picks whoever has waited longest for a turn. When it does not have enough people, it fills the most important roles first and tells you where it had to bend a rule.

Your current settings

1. What you tell it

  • Who can do what. The duty checkboxes on each employee card. Typing means they can work a typing station.
  • What each station is for. The checkboxes on each station card. A role can only sit at a station that allows it.
  • How many of each role you need. The Required number for each role, per day.
  • Who is out, and when. Days off on the employee cards, for any week you pick on the calendar button.

2. At the start of each week

  1. Count the openings. Each role needs its Required number of people, less anyone frozen in that role.
  2. Supervisor overrides come first. Anyone you gave a duty for the week (the pencil button on the schedule, or Assigned Duty on their card) gets it, even if they are resting, over the Fairness Duty Limiter or not ticked for it.
  3. Anyone who owes a week goes next. A person who held a role but was out three or more days of that week is put back on the same role.
  4. Fixed-station staff take a role their station allows, if one is open and they can be picked.
  5. The rest are filled in hierarchy order. Higher roles pick before lower ones. Roles marked equal take turns, one position each.
  6. The fairest person is picked. Among those who can be picked, the one who has gone longest without holding any weekly position goes first. Ties go to whoever has worked the fewest special-duty days overall.
  7. Everyone gets a station. Position holders get a station that allows their role. Everyone else gets a typing station, and those who have been left Unassigned most often get one first.

3. Who can be picked

For a role in a given week, a person must:

  • be ticked for that duty,
  • not be resting from that role,
  • be under the Fairness Duty Limiter,
  • be out no more than two days that week,
  • not be frozen, and not be marked "No special duty" for the week.

If nobody meets all of these, the scheduler does not leave the role empty. It takes the person closest to finishing their rest, then someone over the Fairness Duty Limiter, and shows a warning naming them.

4. Each day

  1. Anyone marked out shows as Out.
  2. Frozen employees keep their position.
  3. One-day overrides are applied. A person you overrode for that day takes the duty you chose; whoever loses the position works a typing station for the day.
  4. Position holders work their role at their own station.
  5. If a holder is out, a stand-in covers for that day only. The stand-in comes from the holder's break group when possible, is chosen by the same fairness order, uses the holder's station, and goes back to their own job the next day.
  6. Everyone else works their typing station. A person without one takes a station that is free that day, or shows as Unassigned.

5. Rest, limits and rotation

  • Max Rot is the most days in a row on one role. With 5, a person does one week and rotates off. With 10, they may stay a second week.
  • Break Rot is how many days a person rests from that role afterwards. Only days they are present count, so a day out does not shorten the rest.
  • The Fairness Duty Limiter caps special-duty days across all roles, so nobody goes from one duty straight into another week after week.
  • The call-out rule. A holder who is out three or more days of their week earns no rest and gets the same role again the next week.
  • Rest that cannot be sustained is shortened. If a rest period is longer than the number of qualified people can cover, it is cut for that day and a warning says by how much.

6. Breaks

Every employee belongs to break group A, B or C. Each group has its own AM break, lunch and PM break. The groups move to the next set of times each week, so nobody keeps the same lunch hour permanently.

7. After-lunch swap

Information Desk and Class C/M staff trade places after lunch, one for one. A pair must each be ticked for the other's duty, and partners in the same break group are matched first. Anyone left without a partner is told to check with a supervisor. There is no swap on a blocked day or a half day.

8. The office calendar

  • A blocked day is nobody's work day. It counts toward no one's duty, rest or days out.
  • Half days and special hours are notes only. They appear on the schedules but do not change who works where.

9. The forecast

The Next Duty column, the 🔜 icon, the 4-Week View and the Employee View are all worked out the same way: the scheduler plays the schedule forward 30 work days on a copy of the roster, using the days off, assigned duties and calendar entries already planned. It updates the moment any of those change.

11. Appointments to publish

The appointment groups in Settings turn the staff schedule into a number of appointments to post for each day. Nothing here changes who works where.

  1. Place the staff, minute by minute. For each group, the people the schedule puts in its ticked positions that day. Typing means anyone at a typing counter. A person is left out while on their break or lunch, on a day out, and during any part-day time off set on their card. A paired Information Desk or Class C/M person counts in their partner's position once their own lunch is over.
  2. Add up each hour. Only the group's hours for the day count (appointment hours or road test hours). A blocked day gives nothing; a half day or special hours shorten the day.
  3. Share each hour by weight. Each appointment type or sub-group takes its weight's share. A sub-group adds the minutes of its own ticked positions, if it has any, and shares the total out again.
  4. Divide by the transaction time. An appointment is counted in the hour its minutes are complete, and whatever is left of an hour carries into the next. A day's total is its minutes divided by the time limit, rounded down.

The page shows a whole week by day, or any one day hour by hour. The hourly table also lists how many people are on break or lunch and how many are off in each hour.

Weights can be read two ways, chosen in Settings. As a share of appointments, 40% Long and 60% Short posts 4 Long for every 6 Short. As a share of staff time, Long gets 40% of the minutes, which is fewer appointments because each one takes longer.

Weights in a group that add up to less than 100 leave the rest of its time unposted, for walk-ins or catching up. Weights over 100 are scaled down to fit.
